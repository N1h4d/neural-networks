ANN: Artificial Neural Network

Layers of fully connected neurons. Choose inputs and targets, then train: the network runs forward, computes the loss, backpropagates the error and updates all 36 weights and 10 biases.

a = f(W·x + b) | W ← W − η·∂L/∂W

Math

a¹ = tanh(W¹x + b¹)
a² = tanh(W²a¹ + b²)
ŷ  = σ(W³a² + b³)
L  = ½ Σ (ŷ − y)²
δ³ = (ŷ − y)·ŷ(1−ŷ)
δˡ = (Wˡ⁺¹ᵀ δˡ⁺¹) ⊙ (1 − aˡ²)
∂L/∂Wˡ = δˡ·(aˡ⁻¹)ᵀ

What is used

3 inputs, two hidden layers of 4 tanh neurons, 2 sigmoid outputs, backpropagation with gradient descent.

Real use: predicting prices, customer churn and credit risk from table data.

What is found and how

The weights that make the outputs match the targets. Each step moves every weight a little against its gradient, so L falls. Raise η to learn faster, but too high makes it jump around.

2CNN: recognizes lines you draw

Click or drag on the 8×8 grid. Four 3×3 filters scan it, then a small trained layer decides: vertical, horizontal or diagonal. Press Train first.







Math
f(i,j) = ReLU(Σ I[i+u, j+v]·K[u,v])
pool = max over each 2×2 block
z_k = Σ w_kq·f_q + b_k
p = softmax(z)    L = −log p_true


What is used
4 fixed line filters, ReLU, 2×2 max-pooling and a trainable softmax layer (108 weights) trained on 240 generated noisy line images.
Real use: the same convolution and pooling idea inside YOLO (car speed), U-Net (park zones, satellite maps) and FaceNet (Face ID).


What is found and how
Training learns which filter responses mean which class: a vertical line lights the vertical filter, so its weights grow. Accuracy is measured on 90 unseen images; your own drawings are the real test. In big CNNs the filters are learned too, here they are fixed to keep the mechanism visible.







3RNN: learns a letter pattern


Each row is one time step. Squares are the hidden state (the memory): amber positive, blue negative. The arrow is the letter the network predicts next.




















Math
h(t) = tanh(Wxh·x(t) + Whh·h(t−1) + bh)
p(t) = softmax(Why·h(t) + by)
Backpropagation through time,
Adagrad, gradients clipped to ±5


What is used
A letter vocabulary, 10 hidden units, the same weights reused at every step.
Real use: LSTM/GRU versions forecast F1 lap times and tyre wear, and power speech recognition and older translation systems.


What is found and how
In “hello” the letter “l” must be followed by “l” the first time and “o” the second time. The input is identical, so only the hidden state can tell them apart. Watch the squares differ on those two rows and the confidence rise.







4GAN: forger vs. detective


Green curve: real data. Amber bars: the generator’s fakes. Dark line: how real the discriminator thinks each value is (0 to 1).




















Math
G(z) = a·z + b,  z ~ N(0,1)
D(x) = σ(w₁u + w₂u² + c),  u = x/5
D maximizes log D(real) + log(1 − D(G(z)))
G maximizes log D(G(z))


What is used
Two models trained against each other: a 2-parameter generator and a 3-parameter discriminator (trained 5 times per generator step to stay stable). Real data are drawn from N(μ, 1.25²).
Real use: synthetic faces, image super-resolution, extra training data.


What is found and how
The generator never sees real numbers, only the discriminator’s feedback, yet its mean b and spread |a| move toward μ and 1.25. The dark line flattens toward 0.5 when fakes become indistinguishable. Move μ while training and the forger follows.







5What happens when you send a prompt


A chat model is a very large Transformer (a descendant of the sequence idea behind RNNs). Nothing is learned from your message at that moment: the finished network only runs forward.




1 · Tokenize
Text is cut into tokens and turned into integer IDs.


2 · Embed
Each ID becomes a vector; position information is added.


3 · Self-attention
Every token weighs every other token, layer after layer.


4 · Predict
Softmax gives a probability for every possible next token.


5 · Sample
One token is chosen (temperature controls randomness).


6 · Repeat
The token is appended and steps 3–5 repeat until the reply ends.
